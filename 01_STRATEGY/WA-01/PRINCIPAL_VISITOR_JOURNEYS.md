# Principal Visitor Journeys

These journeys define planning intent, not final page routes. They should be
validated during WA-02 information architecture.

## 1. First orientation

**Need:** Understand what New Earth is and where to begin.

**Path:** Home -> Start Here -> Civilizational Vision -> Manifesto -> Blueprint.

**Success:** The visitor can explain the project in simple terms and choose a
next path without encountering internal system language too early.

## 2. Doctrine to structure

**Need:** Move from worldview into a credible framework.

**Path:** Manifesto -> Blueprint -> 12 Pillars -> Inner Blueprint -> Transition
Path -> New Earth Test.

**Success:** The visitor understands that the Blueprint connects inner
development, civilizational principles, and practical system design.

## 3. Framework to implementation

**Need:** See how the vision becomes real work.

**Path:** Blueprint -> Systems -> Ecosystem Map -> Projects -> MicroGrow.

**Success:** The visitor understands MicroGrow as an implementation node rather
than an unrelated product and can distinguish public descriptions from private
operational tools.

## 4. Product or project exploration

**Need:** Evaluate a product or project.

**Path:** Projects -> Project detail -> Purpose and status -> Blueprint links ->
Contact or support pathway.

**Success:** The visitor sees ownership, current status, audience, evidence,
and a clear next action. Unready projects remain unlisted or marked as future.

## 5. Founder and creator lanes

**Need:** Understand the people and voices behind the work.

**Path:** About -> Founder's Journey -> Team -> Peter Practical Guides or Hayley
Conscious Living -> relevant Journal or resource content.

**Success:** Biography, authored work, and organisational information are
distinct, consented, and free from duplicate routes.

## 6. Public knowledge

**Need:** Learn, return, and follow the project over time.

**Path:** Journal or Resources -> category/topic -> article or guide -> related
Blueprint, pillar, project, and contact links.

**Success:** Articles and Journal have a clear relationship; content is
discoverable through stable types, categories, authors, and internal links.

## 7. Community and collaboration

**Need:** Join, collaborate, support, or ask a question.

**Path:** Community or Collaborate -> pathway explanation -> eligibility and
expectations -> Contact or approved submission route.

**Success:** The visitor knows what will happen next, what information is
required, and how privacy, safeguarding, and moderation work.

## 8. Trust, accessibility, and legal clarity

**Need:** Confirm the site is credible and usable.

**Path:** Any page -> accessible navigation and footer -> About, Legal, Contact,
FAQ, and glossary.

**Success:** Legal routes are canonical, the footer is not overloaded, content
is readable with assistive technology, and no duplicate policy creates doubt.
