# Brand Visual Direction

## Status

Three proposed directions for Peter review. All tokens remain PROPOSED. No
direction is approved or implemented.

## Direction A - Regenerative Foundation - NOT SELECTED

### Character

Warm, grounded, human, tactile, and community-oriented. It connects the site
to land, stewardship, regeneration, and practical participation.

### Palette and type

Forest greens lead. Warm ivory and sand provide calm surfaces. Restrained gold
is reserved for small accents, rules, or approved highlights. A literary serif
may carry major headings while a neutral sans handles reading.

### Imagery and shape

Use natural textures, generous breathing room, rounded but not playful cards,
quiet borders, and slow transitions. Heroes should feel like a threshold into
place and purpose.

### Advantages

Strongest connection to the Blueprint, community, creator lanes, and
regeneration. Most emotionally accessible for first-time visitors.

### Risks

Can become overly pastoral or soft for credible technology and research pages.
Gold and textured surfaces require strict contrast discipline.

### Suitability

Excellent for Vision, Blueprint, Pillars, About, creator lanes, Community, and
Journal. Suitable for technology when paired with clear diagrams and status
labels.

## Direction B - Living Systems - NOT SELECTED

### Character

Clear, modern, systems-focused, and quietly luminous. It connects ecology,
technology, research, and public explanation without becoming a dashboard.

### Palette and type

Deep green and teal anchor the interface. Light neutral surfaces support dense
explanations. A restrained luminous accent may identify links or evidence states,
never neon text or large glow effects.

### Imagery and shape

Use structured grids, subtle lines, layered diagrams, moderate radii, and precise
spacing. Imagery can combine landscape with close detail and systems diagrams.

### Advantages

Strongest for Ecosystem, Research and Development, public technology, Projects,
and Progress. Makes relationships and evidence easy to scan.

### Risks

Can drift toward generic SaaS or operational-dashboard styling. Systems must
remain public explainers, not interfaces.

### Suitability

Excellent for Ecosystem, technology explainers, research, Projects, Journal
discovery, and status components. Needs warmth from imagery and editorial type.

## Direction C - Earth and Future - APPROVED

### Character

A balanced synthesis: human warmth with credible future-facing clarity. It
supports a civilization story while making practical systems legible.

### Palette and type

Deep forest and earth neutrals form the base. Teal is used selectively for
systems relationships. Warm ivory keeps pages human. Gold is a small ceremonial
accent, never body text. Serif display and privacy-respecting sans reading type
work as a controlled pair.

### Imagery and shape

Cinematic heroes, grounded editorial cards, clear diagrams, moderate radii,
layered but quiet surfaces, and measured motion. Each template can shift emphasis
without changing the core system.

### Advantages

Best full-site balance across Vision, Blueprint, MicroGrow, Research, public
technology, creator lanes, Journal, and future resources. It supports both
meaning and evidence.

### Risks

Requires disciplined art direction to avoid becoming a vague compromise. The
future-facing accent must remain restrained.

### Suitability

Strong across all 38 V2.0 destinations and future V2.x capabilities. It is the
most resilient direction for a long-lived shared system.

## Approval

Peter approved Direction C - Earth and Future on 2026-08-26. Directions A and B
remain in this document as historical comparison evidence and are not selected.
Direction C is the only approved visual direction for implementation planning.
