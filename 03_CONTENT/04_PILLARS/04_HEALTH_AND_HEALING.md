# 04 Health and Healing

## Current distortion

Health systems often treat symptoms late, commercialize illness, and separate mind, body, and spirit.

Prevention is underfunded while expensive interventions drive profit. Mental health is marginalized. Trauma goes unhealed. Health disparities follow lines of wealth and power. The conditions that create chronic disease—stress, poverty, disconnection, poor food, isolation—remain unaddressed.

## New Earth design

Health becomes a lifelong culture of prevention, balance, nourishment, community, and deep healing.

Health is understood as physical, emotional, mental, relational, spiritual, and environmental. Prevention is wiser and more humane than chronic treatment. Trauma healing is central to peacebuilding. Universal access to care means health is a right, not a privilege. Healing communities support whole-person wellness.

## Principles

- Health is physical, emotional, mental, relational, spiritual, and environmental
- Prevention is wiser than chronic treatment
- Trauma healing is central to peacebuilding and healthy communities
- Healthcare is a right, not a market commodity
- Mind, body, and spirit are integrated; they cannot be separated
- Community and belonging are foundations of health

## Features

- **Universal access to care** — Healthcare is guaranteed, with prevention-first and whole-person approaches
- **Prevention as foundation** — Nutrition, movement, sunlight, rest, clean environments, and emotional support are primary health strategies
- **Trauma-informed communities** — Schools, workplaces, and public spaces are designed to prevent re-traumatization and support healing
- **Integration of modalities** — Science-based medicine combined with safe, evidence-based holistic approaches (bodywork, herbal medicine, energy work)
- **Healing centers** — Community spaces for mental health, grief work, meditation, movement, and wholeness
- **Mental health parity** — Emotional and psychological wellbeing receives equal funding and respect as physical health
- **Relational health** — Strong community bonds, meaningful work, and connection to nature are understood as health interventions

## Key questions for implementation

- How do we transition from treatment-based to prevention-based healthcare?
- What's the role of traditional and alternative medicine alongside evidence-based care?
- How do we train providers in trauma-informed, whole-person approaches?
- What infrastructure supports community emotional health?
- How do we address health disparities rooted in historical injustice?

## Connection to other pillars

- **Governance**: Healthcare policy must be transparent and determined by communities, not profit-driven corporations
- **Education**: Teaching children about nutrition, movement, and emotional wellbeing from early on
- **Food and agriculture**: Nutrition is primary medicine; access to healthy food is a health right
- **Housing and habitat**: Safe, beautiful, connected homes and neighborhoods are foundational to health
