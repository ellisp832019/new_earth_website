# 06 Housing and Habitat

## Current distortion

Housing is treated as a speculative commodity rather than a human foundation.

People are displaced by rising rents and property values. Neighborhoods are designed around cars and consumption, not human connection. Neighborhoods are segregated by wealth, isolating both richness and poverty. Beautiful design is expensive; affordable housing is often soulless. Sprawl consumes wildlands. Communities lack spaces for gathering and shared life.

## New Earth design

Every person deserves safe, beautiful, affordable shelter connected to community and nature.

Shelter is a right, not a speculation vehicle. Design affects consciousness; beautiful, human-scaled neighborhoods foster belonging. Communities are mixed-income, walkable, and filled with spaces for gathering. Cities integrate nature and reduce isolation.

## Principles

- Shelter is a human right, not a commodity for speculation
- Design affects consciousness; beautiful space fosters wellbeing and belonging
- Communities should be mixed-income, walkable, and human-scaled
- Neighborhoods should reduce isolation and increase belonging
- Nature should be integrated into urban and suburban design
- Architecture should be elegant, durable, and aligned with local ecology

## Features

- **Affordable housing by design** — Mixed-income neighborhoods where affordability is built in, not added as an afterthought
- **Mixed-use, walkable neighborhoods** — Residential, commercial, gathering, and green space integrated so people can meet daily needs locally
- **Community-centered design** — Public squares, community gardens, shared kitchens, and gathering spaces foster connection
- **Green building** — Materials that are sustainable and healthy; energy-efficient and water-conscious systems
- **Intergenerational housing** — Spaces for families, elders, shared living arrangements, and diverse household structures
- **Nature integration** — Parks, green corridors, food forests, and habitat corridors throughout cities
- **Car-reduced design** — Good public transit, walkability, and cycling infrastructure reduce dependence on personal vehicles

## Key questions for implementation

- How do we create affordable housing without subsidy dependency?
- What prevents gentrification as neighborhoods become desirable?
- How do we redesign suburbs and sprawling areas toward walkability?
- What role does architecture and beauty play in community wellbeing?
- How do we integrate nature into dense urban areas?

## Connection to other pillars

- **Economics**: Mixed-income design requires economic systems that support shared prosperity
- **Health and healing**: Human-scaled, connected neighborhoods support mental and physical health
- **Food and agriculture**: Urban and suburban gardens should be integral to neighborhoods
- **Environment**: Wildlife corridors and nature integration restore biodiversity in human communities
