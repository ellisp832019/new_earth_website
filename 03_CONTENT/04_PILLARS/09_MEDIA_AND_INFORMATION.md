# 09 Media and Information

## Current distortion

Media often amplifies fear, outrage, manipulation, distraction, and division.

Journalism is often funded by corporate interests or advertisers. Algorithms amplify engagement over truth. Misinformation spreads faster than correction. Privacy is invading; data is harvested and weaponized. Power brokers shape narratives from behind the scenes. Citizens are fragmented into competing information ecosystems, unable to find shared reality.

## New Earth design

Information systems become truth-serving, educational, balanced, and conscious.

Information shapes collective reality. Truth is a public good. Journalism should elevate understanding, not weaponize attention. Independent media is protected. Media literacy is taught. Sources are transparent. Public platforms serve community dialogue, not corporate engagement metrics.

## Principles

- Information shapes collective reality; truth is a public good
- Journalism should elevate understanding and enable wise action
- Facts are verifiable and should be transparent about sources
- Diverse perspectives enrich understanding; disinformation fragments it
- Media literacy is a foundational skill
- Privacy is a right; data collection should be minimal and transparent

## Features

- **Independent journalism protected** — Funding and legal protection ensure journalists can investigate without corporate or government interference
- **Media literacy from childhood** — Teaching how to evaluate sources, distinguish fact from opinion, recognize bias and manipulation
- **Incentives aligned with truth** — Removing engagement-based advertising; funding models that reward understanding over outrage
- **Public-interest platforms** — Digital and broadcast platforms designed for education, deliberation, and community connection rather than profit extraction
- **Fact transparency and source standards** — All significant claims include visible sources; corrections are as prominent as original reporting
- **Regulated advertising** — Manipulation tactics prohibited; transparency required about who is speaking and why
- **Diversity of voices** — Ensuring minority perspectives, indigenous knowledge, and critical voices are part of public conversation

## Key questions for implementation

- How do we fund quality journalism without corporate or government control?
- What prevents state censorship while stopping disinformation?
- How do we help people navigate information overload and find shared truth?
- What role do algorithms play in a healthy information ecosystem?
- How do we hold media itself accountable for accuracy and bias?

## Connection to other pillars

- **Governance**: Transparent information is essential for participatory democracy
- **Education**: Media literacy enables citizens to evaluate claims and participate wisely
- **Health and healing**: Accurate health information saves lives; misinformation spreads disease
- **Justice**: Investigative journalism holds power accountable; truth-telling supports healing
