# 02 Economics

## Current distortion

The economy rewards extraction, debt dependency, artificial scarcity, and accumulation without responsibility.

Wealth concentrates among those who own capital while labor is undervalued. Value is measured only in monetary terms, making care work, teaching, healing, and ecosystem restoration invisible. Growth is treated as an end in itself, disconnected from human wellbeing or planetary limits.

## New Earth design

The economy becomes a system of circulation, sufficiency, creativity, and regeneration.

Wealth is a tool for enabling life, not a god to be worshipped. Enough for all before excess for a few. Labor is dignified. Value includes care, healing, teaching, growing food, restoring ecosystems, and building community. Economic success is measured by wellbeing, resilience, trust, and ecological health—not endless growth.

## Principles

- Enough for all before excess for a few
- Wealth is a tool, not a measure of worth
- Labor must be dignified and fairly valued
- Value includes care, healing, teaching, growing food, restoring ecosystems, and building community
- Economic systems must work within planetary limits, not attempt to transcend them
- Cooperation and creativity matter as much as competition

## Features

- **Universal access to basic needs** — Housing, food, clean water, healthcare, and education are guaranteed or highly subsidized for all
- **Regenerative businesses and cooperative ownership** — Worker ownership, benefit corporations, and triple-bottom-line models replace exploitative wage labor where possible
- **Local economies strengthened** — Communities develop local food, energy, and goods production to increase resilience and fairness
- **Ethical global trade** — International exchange is based on fair wages, safe conditions, and environmental protection—not profit maximization
- **Finance redirected toward restoration** — Investment flows toward housing, education, health, food security, and ecological restoration
- **New measures of success** — GDP replaced by indices of wellbeing, resilience, trust, environmental health, and human flourishing

## Key questions for implementation

- How do we transition from debt-based to sufficiency-based thinking?
- What mechanisms prevent wealth from reconcentrating?
- How do we value invisible work (care, community, healing)?
- What's the role of market mechanisms versus cooperative structures?
- How do we manage economic transition without causing hardship?

## Connection to other pillars

- **Governance**: Fair economic allocation requires transparent, community-controlled decision-making
- **Education**: Economic literacy and cooperative skills must be taught to build healthy systems
- **Food and agriculture**: Local food systems reduce dependence and increase community resilience
- **Housing**: Affordable housing design as a right, not a commodity for speculation
