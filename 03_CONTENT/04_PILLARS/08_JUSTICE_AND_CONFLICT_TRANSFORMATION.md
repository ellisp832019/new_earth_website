# 08 Justice and Conflict Transformation

## Current distortion

Justice systems are often punitive, unequal, and detached from healing.

Mass incarceration disproportionately affects the poor and people of color. Punishment does not heal harm or prevent recurrence. Victims are often retraumatized. Offenders are dehumanized rather than transformed. Root causes of harm—poverty, trauma, addiction, exclusion—go unaddressed. The wealthy and powerful avoid accountability while the vulnerable are imprisoned.

## New Earth design

Justice becomes restorative, protective, truthful, and healing-centered.

Harm must be addressed, and accountability matters. But healing and restoration should be pursued wherever possible. Society must address root causes, not just punish outcomes. Protection from violent harm remains serious; the process is humane and wise.

## Principles

- Harm must be addressed and accountability required
- Accountability need not be punitive; healing and restoration are possible
- Root causes of harm—poverty, trauma, addiction, exclusion—must be addressed
- Victims deserve support, truth, and repair
- Offenders deserve the possibility of transformation
- Protection from violent harm is a priority, pursued with respect for all parties
- Justice systems should be transparent, fair, and protect against corruption

## Features

- **Restorative justice practices** — Approaches that bring together those harmed, those who caused harm, and community to address harm and repair relationships
- **Community mediation systems** — Accessible, local conflict resolution before escalation to formal justice
- **Healing centers for trauma recovery** — Support for both victims and offenders to process trauma and build healthy futures
- **Serious but humane protection** — When protection from violent harm is necessary, processes are respectful, monitored, and oriented toward eventual reintegration
- **Structural attention to prevention** — Addressing poverty, abuse, addiction, homelessness, and exclusion that drive harm
- **Truth commissions** — Process for acknowledging historical and ongoing injustices, with transparency and accountability
- **Rehabilitation and reintegration** — Offenders are prepared for return to community; communities support their reintegration

## Key questions for implementation

- How do we transition from punitive to restorative justice without victim abandonment?
- What happens when harm is severe or happening repeatedly?
- How do we ensure powerful people face real accountability?
- What supports do victims need for genuine healing?
- How do we prevent cycles of harm without mass imprisonment?

## Connection to other pillars

- **Health and healing**: Trauma recovery is central to justice; healing is inseparable from accountability
- **Governance**: Justice systems must be transparent and accountable to communities, not centralized power
- **Education**: Teaching conflict resolution, empathy, and nonviolence from early childhood prevents future harm
- **Economics**: Addressing poverty and exploitation removes conditions that drive much harmful behavior
