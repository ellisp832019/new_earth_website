# 10 Environment and Relationship with Nature

## Current distortion

Earth is treated as a warehouse of resources and a dumping ground for consequences.

Ecosystems are destroyed for short-term profit. Species vanish. Oceans are poisoned. Air and water are polluted. Climate destabilizes. Indigenous lands are seized. Earth's suffering is externalized—hidden from the people who benefit from extraction. Humans have spiritually separated from nature, forgetting that we depend on living systems.

## New Earth design

Nature is recognized as a living system to which humanity belongs.

Human life depends on ecological integrity. Rivers, forests, oceans, and species are not expendable. Restoration is a civilizational duty. Humans are part of nature, not separate from it. Economic and policy decisions measure long-term ecological impact.

## Principles

- Human life depends on ecological integrity
- Rivers, forests, oceans, wetlands, and species have intrinsic value and right to exist
- Restoration is a civilizational duty
- Humans are part of nature, not separate or superior to it
- Long-term ecological health must be measured in all major decisions
- Indigenous knowledge and stewardship practices deserve deep respect

## Features

- **Legal protection for vital ecosystems** — Rivers, forests, wetlands, and species are legally protected with enforceable rights
- **Large-scale restoration** — Reforestation, soil repair, watershed restoration, and biodiversity corridors are major civilizational projects
- **Circular material systems** — Eliminating waste by designing for reuse, repair, and regeneration
- **Regenerative resource use** — Harvesting only what can be regenerated; allowing recovery time for ecosystems
- **Cities integrated with nature** — Urban areas are designed as habitats with green corridors, native plants, and wildlife
- **Ecological accounting** — All major decisions include long-term impact on soil, water, air, and living systems
- **Indigenous partnership** — Respecting and learning from indigenous peoples' stewardship practices and land relationships

## Key questions for implementation

- How do we restore ecosystems at the scale needed for climate stabilization?
- What's the role of rewilding and land return?
- How do we transition industries away from extraction toward regeneration?
- How do we measure and protect ecosystem health in real time?
- What does it mean to honor nature as sacred rather than as resource?

## Connection to other pillars

- **Health and healing**: Ecological health and human health are inseparable; clean air, water, and soil are medicine
- **Food and agriculture**: Regenerative food systems restore ecosystems while nourishing people
- **Energy and technology**: Renewable energy and restoration technology must replace extractive systems
- **Culture and spirituality**: Humans must remember our sacred relationship with nature
